Study 00

A quiet page. A busy product.

Your impression is right about page-level choreography. Most section headings, large layouts, changelog entries, quotes, and the footer stay still. Linear concentrates animation in its opening composition and small, convincing product interfaces.

This is a React + Motion + CSS implementation. Motion supplies springs, values, scroll mappings, presence transitions, and shared layout. CSS handles hover responses, navigation keyframes, masks, and stepped SVG loaders. Native scrolling supplies gestures and snapping.

Study 01

Blur, lift, and a tiny stagger.

Desktop headline lines start at 400ms and 500ms; the description follows at 600ms. All use the same 1000ms tween: opacity 0→1, blur 10→0px, and translateY 20→0% of the element’s own height. Small overlap makes two lines feel like one thought.

The desktop announcement waits until 2000ms. Mobile has four headline lines spaced by 33ms, and an 850ms announcement delay. The intro gate skips the hidden state for reduced motion, hash navigation, or a previously seen hero key; first scroll can terminate intro gating.

Study 03

Lift the object—and its mask.

The purpose illustration uses five SVG layers. Thick, invisible stroke hit areas select a layer. Layers at or before that index lift by 31.667px; selection is capped at index 3. A 50ms cascade spreads the response through the stack.

The 700ms curve is cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1). Matching black shapes inside the SVG mask also move to preserve occlusion. Moving only the visible outlines exposes lines through the stack.

Study 04

Pointer distance becomes geometry.

The speed illustration contains 15 slender SVG slabs. Pointer x maps into a 272-unit viewBox. Each slab calculates a height from its distance to an anchor, then approaches that target with a spring.

The SVG path’s d value is recomputed from that height. Source damping is 18, mass 1; the bundled spring engine defaults to stiffness 100. Leaving restores an uneven idle profile. This is not a CSS hover scale.

Study 09

Scroll moves the light, not the card.

Product panels reuse a border masked by a radial gradient. Motion maps the target passing from “start at viewport center” to “end at viewport center” onto mask y 0→100%. The selected edge uses x 0% or 100%.

A second mechanism sets the background y offset to the negative of the panel’s bounding-rectangle top. This compensates for the panel’s movement and makes the glow appear attached to the viewport. Static masks feather scenes into the page; those masks are compositing, not animations.

Study 10

Insertion, reaction, reflow, transfer.

The intake illustration starts with a prefilled command and blinking cursor. Send updates local React state. The user message grows from height 0, fades in and rises 32px with a 500ms zero-bounce spring. A reaction arrives after 900ms: scale .3→1, rotate 45→0°, y 4→0, with a 700ms spring and bounce .425.

After 2000ms the response enters over 600ms. Another 1000ms later, two issues enter Todo and push existing cards down. New cards use 600ms zero-bounce springs and 1200ms highlight fades. A later local “start work” command moves cards into In Progress through matching shared-layout IDs.

Motion earns its place here: measured reflow and shared layout preserve continuity when content changes size or parent. CSS can reproduce a fixed sequence, but reactive layout needs measurement and orchestration.

Study 12

A conversation has a rhythm.

The four-panel stage keeps its first and last panels static, while active middle conversations run. It prepares them with a 400px viewport margin. Prompts use a 300ms zero-bounce spring, opacity,10px rise and 2px blur. Response rows use 8px rise with the same blur and spring.

Thinking starts at 400ms; response rows begin at 2400ms, spaced 300ms apart. The sequence settles after another 2400ms. Scenario replacement starts 4000ms after completion, with an extra 1200ms on one middle panel. AnimatePresence mode="wait" fades out then in sequentially, each 350ms.

A masked overlay sweeps over thinking text. A ResizeObserver scrolls the chat to the newest content. These are separate clocks; “the panel fades in” misses most of the choreography.

Study 14

Native gestures, spring-driven controls.

Mobile principles and quotes use horizontal overflow, mandatory snap, start alignment and scroll-snap-stop:always. There is no autoplay. Gesture inertia comes from the browser.

The reusable arrow helper animates scroll position with stiffness 100/damping 20, disabling snap during the spring. A source click requests three items; hold repeats one-item requests every 500ms. The inspected mobile homepage did not expose arrows; study-only controls below let you compare the helper with direct gestures.

Study 16

What to borrow—and how to build it.

CSS is enough

Hover colors, panel slides, menu fades, masks, dot frames and a duplicated marquee.

Motion earns its place

Geometry springs, reactive mappings, presence sequencing, measured reflow and cross-parent identities.

The browser helps

Gesture scrolling, snapping, editing and caret motion. Preserve native behavior.

Keep a small opening sequence for orientation, then tie motion to an interaction or product state. Reuse curves and durations. Static grain, gradients, cropping and feathered masks provide much of the richness without requiring entire scenes to move.

Use IntersectionObserver/useInView to decide WHEN to run.

Fewer repeated page entrances: yes. Fewer meaningful mechanisms: not necessarily. Linear chooses where to spend motion, and how little needs to move at once.
